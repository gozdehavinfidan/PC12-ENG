IntelliCell · model research round 2 · 2026-09-27

From one Swin model to a soma-and-neurite system that learns from the unlabelled pool

The TÜSEB plan bets accuracy on a bigger architecture. With about 70 labelled images, the evidence says accuracy comes from three other places: a teacher adapted to our own microscopy, labels that are cheap enough to have many more of, and losses and output heads that care about neurite topology. This page ranks what to adopt, what to trial and what to drop, with a source for every item.

Bottom line

Eight changes, ordered by expected accuracy gain per week of work. Each is justified further down and in the catalogue.

  1. Drop the Swin-Transformer trunk; deploy a CNN student distilled from a microscopy-adapted foundation teacher. Continue DINOv2/v3 self-supervised training on our unlabelled PC12 fields (DINOCell: +10.4% SEG over SAM-based models on LIVECell), use it as a GPU-only teacher, and distil into an nnU-Net ResEnc or MedNeXt-S student that runs fast in ONNX on CPU. The "nnU-Net Revisited" benchmark shows transformer superiority claims mostly vanish under matched compute, and a Swin trained on 70 images will memorise.
  2. Turn the unlabelled pool into supervision. Use foundation-model-teacher semi-supervised training (ESRVS keeps 93.7% of fully supervised Dice with 1–10% labels; CorrMatch reaches 76% mIoU with 92 labels) instead of one-shot pseudo-labels.
  3. Use the fluorescence you already acquire as free dense labels. In-silico labelling pretraining (phase → fluorescence regression, non-GAN) gives pixel supervision on every paired field, not just the 70 hand-drawn ones.
  4. Label neurites with scribbles or skeleton strokes, not outlines. For a 2–3 px tube, a stroke is the label. This buys 3–5× more labelled images for the same hours, which beats any architecture change at this sample size.
  5. Predict geometry, not just masks. Add heads for a neurite signed-distance/centreline map, a junction heatmap and a soma distance map, and feed a Frangi vesselness channel. Length, branch count and angles then come from predicted geometry instead of brittle post-hoc skeletonisation.
  6. Pre-register the topology-loss A/B. Compare soft-clDice, Skeleton Recall (≈90% cheaper), cbDice and Topograph on our folds. A 2025 re-evaluation found skeleton losses often fail to beat Dice+CE, so this must be measured on our data, not assumed.
  7. Make the NTI a glass-box model with guarantees. Use pycytominer normalisation → mixed-effects correction for plate and replicate → a monotone-constrained EBM or shape-constrained GAM, whose exact Shapley decomposition replaces KernelSHAP. Add benchmark concentrations and conformal intervals on top.
  8. Ship an offline, citation-checked RAG explainer. Qwen3-8B (GGUF, Apache-2.0) + BGE-M3 hybrid retrieval + a reranker + sqlite-vec, over a licence-clean corpus led by AOP-Wiki (598 AOPs, CC BY-SA), the OECD DNT guidelines, and the 5.3M CC-BY articles in the PMC open-access set. Every sentence must cite a shipped chunk, or it is dropped.
Honest limit: the gains quoted here come from other datasets (LIVECell, retinal vessels, CT/MRI). None is a promise for PC12 phase contrast. They tell us what to test first. The 5-fold mean ± std on our own folds is the only number that decides.

Metrics to add to the TÜSEB set

Keep soma IoU ≥ 0.85, neurite Dice ≥ 0.80 and length MAE ≤ 5 µm. Add Betti-0/Betti-1 error, junction F1 (detected branch points within 3 px), clDice as a metric, and per-feature interval coverage. Dice can look fine while a neurite is broken into three pieces. That breaks count, length and angle, which are the values the NTI actually uses.

NTI statistics

The index is where a small NN plus KernelSHAP is weakest: few rows, a dose design with structure, and a claim the reviewers will test.

1 · Normalise per plate

pycytominer robust-z to vehicle wells, and feature QC before modelling. Plate drift then cannot pass for a dose effect.

2 · Model replicate structure

A linear mixed model with random intercepts for plate and replicate (lme4 / statsmodels). ARMED only if a neural head survives. Treating wells as i.i.d. inflates significance.

3 · Glass-box index

An EBM with monotone constraints (CGA2M+) or a scam GAM. The constraint sign is set per compound: curcumin is protective, and watch for H₂O₂ hormesis at low dose. Exact additive Shapley replaces KernelSHAP.

4 · Potency, not just score

A model-averaged benchmark concentration per parameter (BMDExpress 2 logic, or Bayesian BMD with bridge sampling) sits alongside the 4PL fit. ToxPi/Pareto integration follows the 2026 OECD-aligned DNT battery.

5 · Intervals with guarantees

CV+ conformal intervals, stratified by time point because exchangeability breaks across 0/6/24 h. Use Mondrian conformal if a toxic / non-toxic call is ever made.

6 · Leak-proof validation

Leave-one-dose-out (as in TÜSEB), leave-one-replicate-out, and cluster-aware splits so that fields from one well never straddle folds.

Guardrails that make it safe for a toxicity tool

  • Cite-or-drop: the LLM emits [chunk-id] spans. Each claim is checked against its cited chunk (BGE-M3 similarity), and unsupported claims are removed before display (ALCE metrics).
  • Authority tiers in retrieval: OECD digest and AOP-Wiki rank above PMC articles, which rank above textbook definitions. Tiers are kept as metadata filters in the same SQLite file.
  • Numbers never come from the LLM: NTI, doses and CIs are injected from the result JSON (the mock:true flag included), and the LLM only explains them.
  • Evaluation: a private 50–100-question gold set built from TG endpoints and AOPs, scored with RAGAS as a regression test between versions (the small local judge is noisy, so scores are relative only).
  • Phase 2: LightRAG graph retrieval seeded from the AOP-Wiki graph for multi-hop "compound → MIE → key event → neurite loss" answers.

How this was made

  • Baseline: the TÜSEB method section (TUSEB/bolumler/07_yontem.txt) and the first research round (llm-wiki/16, 08, 15). Everything already covered there (plain U-Net family, clDice, Cellpose-SAM, deep ensembles, IG) was excluded, so this round had to find new items.
  • Search: local-LLM workers (omp, ai-lab/local-llm, web search), each assigned one disjoint topic with a fixed JSON schema, followed by a gap round for books and corpus counts.
  • Verification: two independent Sonnet agents opened every source URL. Corrections they found (missing code repos, moved repos) are applied in the catalogue.
  • Synthesis: ranking, architecture and the old → new mapping are Claude's judgement over the verified items. Where a paper states no number, the catalogue says "unverified" instead of inventing one.
  • Raw data: llm-wiki/_research/R7–R13.json, G1–G2.json.
